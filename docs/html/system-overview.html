How a lab run stays true

An executor runs workflows on three simulated instruments: a liquid handler, an incubator and a plate reader. Messages can be lost, late or repeated, and any process can crash. The goal is a run record that never claims something the evidence doesn't support.

One rule shaped the designDurable facts, short-lived intentions, and Postgres decides. Commands may be lost and are simply sent again. Facts about what happened are stored until the executor has saved them. Every decision is one database transaction.

        What this design does not promise

        • Not exactly-once delivery. Messages can arrive twice, late, or not at all. Stable job ids, JetStream's duplicate window (2 min) and idempotent apply make repeats harmless. A job runs at most once on its instrument, assuming one driver per instrument and a ledger that survives restarts (K55).
        • Not guaranteed to finish. A driver that never comes back (K6), or a lost read whose re-reads also fail (K39), leaves its run running for ever. That is true, but stuck: the operator action to end it was not built.
        • Not instant. After the executor is cut off from NATS, the record can lag by up to the 30s ack wait (K62). A storage outage costs at least SeaweedFS's restart, about 16s. measured
        • Assumptions. One executor, one NATS server, one driver per instrument (K55, K56). Which operations are safe to repeat on real equipment is a guess, so it is a setting per device, not code. guess
