Hang and retry policy, v2

What ends a step that cannot succeed as it stands, and when we try it again. Decided as D4: option B. Built from v1 (tmp/d4-hang-retry.html, kept as it was). Nothing here is built yet.

What changed from v1. v1 treated a retryable failure, a hang and lost data as one "retry trigger", and took a step's status from its latest attempt. That is wrong for lost data: under D3 the first attempt is still open, because its upload may still land. v2:
  1. separates the three causes by is attempt 1 finished? and is repeating it safe? (§3);
  2. decides a step from all its attempts, not the latest (§6);
  3. adds a timeline for a lost read and its re-read (§8);
  4. records the choices made: B, final hang is failed, defaults (§12).

1. The problem: three ways a step gets stuck

Most step endings are already handled: completed (with verified data, for a read) and failed (the instrument reported an error). Three cases are not:

Hung

The instrument hangs, frees itself after 30s and writes hung in its ledger. Our driver reports finished(hung). Today the step stays unresolved (API: running) forever.

Data lost

The plate reader completed the read, but the bytes died with a driver restart. Under D3 the read stays unresolved in case a late upload lands. Nothing ever ends it.

Retryable failure

The instrument reported an error and said retrying makes sense. Today the step is failed and the run fails anyway.

Claimed but never started (the driver died between the claim and starting the instrument) is not part of this decision. Nothing physical happened, so the fix is to resend the same job, not to start a new attempt. That is reconcile (S7).

Why this matters for truth. A step that says running forever after the instrument stopped is a claim that is no longer true. A careless retry can be physically wrong: if a hung liquid handler did dispense, a retry dispenses twice. The policy has to be both live (runs end) and safe (nothing is repeated that should not be).

2. Words we need

Attempt
One try at one step. Attempt 1 is the first; a retry is attempt 2, and so on.
Job id
run/step/attempt, e.g. run-8c36/read_plate/2. A repeated command for the same job never runs twice (the driver answers known). A new attempt has a new job id, so it does run again: that is the point of a retry, and its danger.
Finished attempt new in v2
No evidence about it can still arrive. True once the ledger says failed or hung, or once a read is verified. Not true of a read reported lost: its upload may still land (D3).
Repeat-safe
Doing the device's operation again after an unknown outcome does no harm. Reading a plate only looks. Dispensing liquid cannot be undone. Incubating a little longer is probably harmless, but that is a guess about real chemistry.
Re-read on data loss new in v2
A separate question from repeat-safe: is a measurement taken later an acceptable substitute for the lost one? Physically safe is not the same as scientifically equivalent.

3. The three causes are not the same new in v2

Is attempt 1 finished?What do we know physically?Is repeating safe?Can attempt 1 still change?
Retryable failureyes: the ledger says failed it failed, and the instrument says a retry is sensibleyes, on the instrument's own wordno
Hungyes: the ledger says hung unknown: it may have done some or all of the workonly if the device is repeat-safeno
Data lostno (D3) the read happened; only the data is missingthe reader only looks guess for real readersyes: a late upload can land

So each cause gets its own trigger:

  • Retryable failure: retry at once. The instrument is free and has said a retry is sensible.
  • Hung: retry after finished(hung), only on a repeat-safe device. Otherwise the step is failed: "instrument hung; outcome unknown; not repeated".
  • Data lost: re-read at once, alongside the still-open attempt 1, if the device re-reads on data loss. Don't wait for the late upload: D3 found no limit on that wait. The driver already waited its 10s grace before reporting data_lost.

5. The measurement: faults are the same in every run

The instrument picks a job's fate from sha256(seed : device : step : attempt) % 100. The run id is not in it, and attempt counts this run's earlier executions of the step. Under one seed, the first try of a step has the same fate in every run; only a later attempt rolls again. Computed with the instrument's own function, seed 0, at 30%: measured

6. The rule: a step is decided by all its attempts changed in v2

v1 said "a step's status comes from its latest attempt". v2:

  1. completed once any attempt is verified complete. If several are, the first verified one supplies data_ref (D2: a verified completion is never undone). The others stay in the history.
  2. failed only when every attempt is finished, none completed, and no further attempt will be made.
  3. Otherwise running: an attempt is in flight, a retry is due, or a lost read's upload could still land.

A run finishes only when every step is completed or failed by this rule. An attempt that is still open does not hold the run back once another attempt of the same step has completed.

Why "latest attempt" was wrong, case 1

Attempt 1's data is lost, so it is still open. Re-reads 2 and 3 fail. "Latest attempt" marks the step failed, but attempt 1's upload could still land. That is exactly what D3 forbids. Under the v2 rule the step stays running.

Why "every open attempt blocks" is wrong, case 2

Attempt 1's data is lost and the re-read succeeds. If attempt 1 still held the run open, the re-read would buy nothing. Under the v2 rule the step is completed by attempt 2, and attempt 1 stays in the history as "data lost; may still appear".

7. Walk-through: a hung incubation

  1. The executor sends run-x/incubate_samples/1. The driver claims it and starts the instrument.
  2. The instrument hangs. Ledger and record both say running, which is true.
  3. After 30s the instrument frees itself and writes hung. The driver publishes finished(hung).
  4. Attempt 1 is now finished, with "outcome unknown". The incubator is repeat-safe and 1 of 3 attempts is used, so the executor records a retry, attempt 2.
  5. The dispatch loop sends run-x/incubate_samples/2. It has a new job id, so it runs. Claim, run and report work as usual.
  6. Attempt 2 completes, so the step is completed and the run moves on.

On the liquid handler the same story stops at step 4: failed, "instrument hung; outcome unknown; not repeated: liquid handling is not safe to repeat". There is no timer: the retry waits for the instrument's own report that it gave up, so attempt 2 never goes to a frozen instrument.

8. Walk-through: a lost read and its re-read new in v2

  1. Attempt 1 completes on the plate reader. The driver restarts before uploading, waits its grace, and reports data_lost.
  2. Attempt 1 stays open (D3). The plate reader re-reads on data loss, so attempt 2 starts at once, without waiting.
  3. Attempt 2's data is stored and verified. The step is completed, with data_ref and times from attempt 2. The run can finish, because attempt 1 no longer decides anything.
  4. Later, attempt 1's delayed upload lands. The executor's recheck finds it and records it on attempt 1. The step's result stays attempt 2's (D2: the first verified completion is never replaced).
The other ending. If the re-reads also fail, the step stays running: attempt 1's upload could still land. This is the only case left where a step can stay open indefinitely (K39, now narrower). An operator's "abandon" would close it.
A re-read is a different measurement. It is taken later, and the plate may have changed. In this simulator the data even depends on the job id, so the two reads differ. The record must say which attempt the data came from and when. Whether a later re-read is scientifically acceptable is a guess for real labs, which is why "re-read on data loss" is its own setting per device.

  • REST (fixed shape): one status, plus times, error and data_ref from the attempt that decided the step. If none has decided it yet, they come from the latest attempt.
  • GraphQL: exposes attempts, so a scientist can see the first read was lost.
  • Finding a step from an old job id: parse run/step/attempt, find the step, then the attempt. Today's lookup only knows the current attempt, so it would drop late facts for older ones.
  • Ledger vs record: both show two executions. Acceptance check 5 still says "ran more than once" under faults: honest, and explained in NOTES.md (K5).

11. The options we compared

A. Operator decides

No automatic retry; GraphQL retryStep / abandonStep. Safe, but under any fault seed runs never end on their own (§5).

B. Automatic, per device chosen

Per-cause triggers (§3), the all-attempts rule (§6), per-device repeat_safe and reread_on_data_loss, at most 3 attempts.

C. Instrument's flag only

A hang returns no flag, so it can't decide the hardest case. It ends up as A or as an unsafe B.
